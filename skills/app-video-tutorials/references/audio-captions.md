# Narration, captions, and accessible instruction

Read when planning voiceover, on-screen instructions, caption files, or silent tutorials. Primary references are in [the decision guide](guide.md).

## Script and narration

Write spoken instructions for the viewer's task, using the app's actual labels. Explain the action and result in order. Describe essential visual information such as which option is selected; avoid relying entirely on “click here,” color, or cursor movement. Pronounce product names and technical terms consistently.

Prefer supplied or authorized narration. Check available tools before promising generated speech. Keep voice-service credentials out of projects and ensure any external processing is within authorized scope. Do not clone an identifiable person's voice without their explicit authorization. Respect music, voice, font, and footage rights.

Align the timeline to measured speech duration. Leave enough time to read labels and see the resulting app state. If speech is too long, simplify the script or extend the scene rather than clipping words or speeding it until difficult to follow. A requested voice track that cannot be generated is a remaining deliverable, not an invisible success.

## Captions and transcript

Plan captions early so they do not cover controls or confirmations. Use readable contrast, line lengths, safe margins, and consistent positioning. Caption spoken content and meaningful audio information. Correct automatic transcription for names, numbers, UI labels, and punctuation; review synchronization against the final edit.

Choose open/burned-in captions, a sidecar format such as WebVTT/SRT, or both according to the destination. Do not claim selectable captions are embedded merely because a sidecar exists. Provide a transcript with essential actions and outcomes when useful or required. Describe important silent visual events in narration or descriptive text as appropriate to the audience and accessibility target.

For a silent tutorial, write complete instructional text and allow reading time. A sequence of decorative labels is not an equivalent instructional experience. Avoid flashes and excessive motion, and offer a calmer variant or written alternative when the audience needs it.

## Audio review

Listen to the complete rendered export, preferably with headphones and ordinary speakers when available. Check intelligibility, clipping, abrupt cuts, missing phrases, channel balance, and drift after transitions or speed changes. Keep music below speech and apply fades deliberately. Use platform loudness guidance if specified; report any measured targets and tool results rather than inventing a universal value.

Record whether captions were reviewed, audio was actually listened to, and a transcript was delivered. If playback or hearing review is unavailable, mark it NOT VERIFIED and identify the remaining check.
